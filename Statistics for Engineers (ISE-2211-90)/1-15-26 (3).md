*Counting techniques*
often necessary to determine the total # of outcomes in a sample space in order to determine probabilities associated with them
*multiplication rule* 
operation involving k steps 
.# of ways of computing step 1: n1, step 2: n2
.# = n1 * n2 *... * nk

ex: new car; may choose from:
- three different stereos... n1 = 3
- 4 colors...............................n2 = 4
- auto or manual..................n3 = 2
- with or without ac............n4 = 2
if no dependence between he options (all red cars are manual) then the number of possible cars is:
n1 * n2* n3* n4 = 3* 4 * 2 * 2 = 48

*Probability*: quantifies the likelihood or chance that an outcome in the sample space of a random experiment will occur

may range from 0-1 (100%)
the way in which we compute probabilities depends on the nature of the experiment

*Model of equally likely outcomes*
in a sample space of n outcomes, each outcome has the same probability of 1/n

ex: fair six-sided dice
the probability of rolling each number is 1/n
1/6 = 16.7%

ex: say we have a sample space with four outcomes
S { a b c d}
lets say we know 
P(a) = .1 (10%)
P(b) = .3 (30%)
P(c) = .5 (50%)
P(d) = .1 (10%)
define events:
A {a b}
B {b c d}
C {d}
the probability of an even is the sum of the probabilities of the outcomes in that event

P(A) = P(a) + P(b) = .1 +.3 =.4 = 40%

this concept also applies to set operation
P(A') = P{c d} = .5 + .1 = .6 = 60%
P (A ∩ B) = P(b) = .3
P (A ∪ B) = 1 or 100%
verify with a venn diagram
P(A ∩ C) = P{∅} = 0
