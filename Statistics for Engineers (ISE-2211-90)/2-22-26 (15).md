hypothesis testing
often we are tasked with choosing between two competing statements called hypotheses, a population param, such as mu

ex: an ejection seat <u>should</u> have a mean propellant burning rate of 50 cm/s
too low and pilot might not be ejected,
too high and they might be injured
hypothesis are always set up with a null hypothesis:
	Ho= mu = 50 cm/s
	always equality about an unknown population param
	
and an alternative hypothesis
	 H1 = mu NOT = 50 cm/s
how do we test these hypotheses so we know which one to support? 
in this example, H1 suggests that mu could be higher or lower than 50 cm/s
we could have proposed either of two one-sided alt hypothesis:
H1 : mu <50cm/s
OR
H1: mu > 50cm/s

*like upper and lower*
testing hypotheses utilizes information from a random sample of the population
only two possible outcomes:
1. reject the null (Ho)
 OR
 2. fail to reject Ho
we never say that we support a hypothesis or say it's true

we have three methods available for testing hypotheses:
1. confidence interval method
2. fixed significance level method
3. p value method
<u>Tests on mu, sigma known</u>

	test the following hypotheses at the alpha = .05 level of significance

	Ho: mu = 50 cm/s
	H1: mu NOT = 50 cm/s

	test data: a sample of n=25 ejection seats yielded a mean propellant burning rate of xbar = 51.3 cm/s
	known omega = 2 cm/s
51.3 sounds close to 50 right?
